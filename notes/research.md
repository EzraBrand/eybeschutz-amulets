# State of research (21-Nov-2026)

Sources for each claim are in brackets. "[web]" = seen only in a web
summary, not yet checked in the source itself.

## 1. The events

- Eybeschütz was rabbi of Metz (to 1750), then of the Three Communities
  (Altona, Hamburg, Wandsbek, "AHU"). [Liebes]
- He wrote amulets for women in childbirth. In 1750–51, amulets from Metz,
  Frankfurt and Hamburg were opened. Emden read them as Sabbatean, and the
  controversy began in 1751. [web: Jewish Encyclopedia, SHMH Hamburg]
- **Metz:** the community made a four-page notarized copy of five amulets,
  dated 17-Mar-1751, and notarized it again before the French civil
  authorities on 17-Nov-1751. Leiman & Schwarzfuchs found this document and
  published it in 2006. [web: Leiman–Schwarzfuchs abstract, Kotzk Blog]
- The Hamburg Senate supported Emden. Liebes suggests that this was because
  of Danish (Altona) versus Hamburg politics. [Liebes]
- At one point the courts ordered that nothing more be printed about the
  amulets. [web: Jewish Encyclopedia]

## 2. The two readings

**Note (from Leiman–Schwarzfuchs n. 17):** *Sefat Emet* is often given to
Emden, but its author is unknown, possibly Nehemiah Reischer. Where these
notes say "Emden's decoding", that is shorthand for the anti-Eybeschütz
decoders.

**Emden:** the amulets are coded statements of faith in Shabbetai Zevi as
messiah. He used gematria, notarikon, atbash and similar substitutions.
[Sefat Emet; web]

**Eybeschütz:** the texts are only Holy Names, each to be read
separately, with roots in kabbalistic books. They cannot be read as one
text. He gave two commentaries of his own. [web: Wikipedia; Luḥot Edut]
He also said that the copies were misread or tampered with. [Leiman 48]

**A test case (Liebes, p. 9):** an amulet has `עבדו מביחו` followed by
the name `שבתי`, in a slightly changed form. Read `מביחו` as `משיחו` with
ב↔ש, which is the atbash pair, and you get "His servant, His messiah,
Shabbetai". R. Naḥman of Bratslav read `מביחו` instead as the initials of
Gen. 6:8 `ונח מצא חן בעיני ה'`. Liebes calls that reading "not
acceptable" and says it follows Eybeschütz's own defence method.
→ This is a clean, testable example: one atbash swap gives a messianic
phrase. How often does a single swap give a "meaningful" word in control
texts?

Related evidence that scholars use: *Va-Avo ha-Yom el ha-Ayin* (a
Sabbatean kabbalistic text, attributed to Eybeschütz); the open
Sabbatean outbreak around his son Wolf in 1760; the letter from Landau
to Maria Theresa. [Liebes] These are context. They are not part of the
amulet test.

## 4. Gaps we can fill

1. **No statistical test of the decoding rules.** No source that we found
   gives one. The critics' complaint that Emden's reading method changed
   from one occasion to the next was never measured. [web]
2. **No single corpus.** The amulet texts are spread over *Sefat Emet*,
   *Luḥot Edut* (several editions), *Shevirat Luḥot ha-Aven*, the Metz
   notarized copies, and probably *Edut be-Yaakov*. No edition puts them
   side by side with their variants.
3. **No list of the variants between witnesses.** Emden's copy, Eybeschütz's
   printed copy and the Metz notarized copy of the same amulet can be
   compared letter by letter. This also tests the "tampered" claim.
